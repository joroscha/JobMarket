## Benefit Choices

### Medical

The budget uses Aetna POS Employee + Family at $198/month because that is the plan you are leaning toward.

However, the Aetna HDHP deserves a serious look:

- HDHP Employee + Family premium: $0/month
- Employer HSA contribution: $2,000/year
- Family deductible: $4,000
- Family out-of-pocket max: $6,000

Compared with Aetna POS, the HDHP starts ahead by $2,376/year in avoided premiums plus $2,000/year in employer HSA money. That is a $4,376 annual head start before medical usage.

The POS is still reasonable if you prefer predictable copays and a more traditional plan, but the HDHP may be financially better if the network, prescriptions, and expected care look acceptable.

### Dental

Recommendation: start with Dental Base unless you expect major dental work.

| Plan | Monthly Family Cost | Annual Max |
|---|---:|---:|
| Guardian PPO Base | $0 | $2,000 |
| Guardian PPO Buy-Up | $44.22 | $5,000 |

The buy-up costs about $531/year before any tax savings. It is worth considering if someone expects crowns, implants, oral surgery, or other expensive dental work. For routine preventive care and a 2-year-old, base is probably fine.

### Vision

Recommendation: Vision Base unless you expect meaningful glasses/contact lens spend every year.

| Plan | Monthly Family Cost | Exam / Frames Frequency |
|---|---:|---|
| Vision Base | $6 | Frames every 24 months |
| Vision Buy-Up | $42.40 | Frames every calendar year |

The buy-up costs about $436/year more than base before tax savings. Base is probably enough unless you or your wife regularly need new glasses/contacts and the allowance math clearly pays off.

## Pre-Tax Premiums

Employer medical, dental, and vision contributions are commonly deducted pre-tax through a Section 125 cafeteria plan. That usually reduces federal income tax and FICA wages, and often state/local taxable wages, though details can vary by state and employer payroll setup.

For planning, assume the $204/month POS + vision base cost comes out pre-tax. The take-home estimates above model it that way for federal and FICA.

## Life Insurance

Because this is a one-income household with a spouse and child, term life insurance is worth pricing.

Planning placeholder: $50-$100/month.

Reasonable target: roughly $1.5M-$2M of term life, depending on rent, savings, future childcare/education goals, and how long you want to protect your family. A 20- or 30-year term policy is usually the clean first option. Avoid whole life as the default unless there is a specific planning reason.

- With a reasonable Pittsburgh family budget, base salary can likely leave about $3,000/month of cushion before counting the Incentive Mix.
- In Brooklyn at $5,000/month rent, the budget is workable but depends on the annualized incentive or reducing costs like the car.
- I would use base salary for fixed commitments and treat the Incentive Mix as savings, investment, travel, and flexibility money.
